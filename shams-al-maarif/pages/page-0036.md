There are four. Its form is produced from the multiplication of 4 by 4, and the elements are four: fire, air, earth, and water. The four humors are yellow bile (ṣafrāʾ), blood (dam), blood, and black bile (sawdāʾ). In the number four there is the nature (ṭabāʾiʿ) and the power of their equilibrium. The letter dal appears among the eternal names, and it is evident in the Eternal Name, and particularly it is in the name of God, the Most High, al-Wadūd (the Loving). In the Eternal Name it is foremost among all the letters, and in both noble names, Muḥammad and Aḥmad, it is at the end of all. This is an indication that dawām (eternity), that is dal, is its end—it does not have a beginning.

The letter dal is foremost in the divine eternal name because dawām (perpetuity) with the accompaniment of first and last belongs exclusively to God, the Most High. He has made His servants share in dawām of existence (baqāʾ) in the afterlife after this world. This letter dal is the letter of the Throne (ʿArsh), because the Throne has no temporal existence; rather, it is first in the world of creation (ʿālam al-akhṭāʿāt), and it is also first in the world of majesty (ʿālam al-majd). The ascension of spirits is toward it, and within it are the ranks of intellects. The lights of mercy are also within it alone. Some mystics (ʿārifīn) have received the unveiling of this realm of the Throne through exactly this method—similar to what happened with Ḥāriṯah, may God be pleased with him.

When the Messenger of God ﷺ asked him, "O Ḥāriṯah, in what state did you spend your morning?"

He replied, "O Messenger of God, I spent the morning as a true believer."

The Noble Prophet, peace and blessings of God be upon him and his family, asked, "What is the reality of your faith?"

Ḥāriṯah said, "O Messenger of God ﷺ, when I spent the morning, I presented my soul before myself in front of this world, and I saw that gold, stone, atoms, and earth, and silver—all were equal to a pauper. It was as if I were seeing the Divine Throne before my eyes. People were being summoned for the reckoning—some being taken toward Paradise and some toward Hellfire."

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said to Ḥāriṯah, may God be pleased with him: "O Ḥāriṯah, you have recognized your soul, so make it obligatory upon yourself."

The Prophet of God ﷺ said: "When the sacred spirit is in a state of purity, it passes beneath the Throne while performing prostration."

In the letter dal are the mysteries of dawām (perpetuity), maʿiyyat (accompaniment), and baqāʾ (existence). The name of God, the Most High, al-Wadūd, is derived from the root w-w-d. It is ambiguous—if it is apparent, one says wadd (love); if it is hidden, one says ḥubb (affection). The beginning of love (wadd) is affection (maḥabbah). Love has two types: apparent and hidden. Its apparent name is wadd (love) and its hidden name is ḥubb (affection). The dwelling place of love is the heart. Among the stations of the heart, this is the place of the greatest unveiling.

[Heading: On the Condition of the Heart]

Love is something subtle between affection and passionate love (walajjah), whose dwelling place is shagaf (infatuation), and affection is the hidden aspect of love, whose dwelling place is the pure heart, because within the heart there are three cavities (ajwāf). One is toward the upper side, from which it is both sleeping and purified—it is luminous light, and it is the place of Islam.

The meanings of the cavities here are difficult, and this is the place of the power of memory (quwwat al-ḥāfiẓah) in human beings, which organizes the meanings of the emerging spirits. The second cavity is in the middle of the heart, another luminous place—it is the station of remembrance (dhikr). This is the place of iṭmīnān (tranquility) and the thoughts of the spirit.

The third cavity is deep within the heart. This portion is the softest and most delicate of the entire heart. It is called the fuʾād. It is also the place of grace, knowledge, light, beauty, and secrets. Its true origin is the place of the delicacies of wisdom (ḥikamah) and the good life (ḥayāt ṭayyibah) that is produced from the cardiac heat, and this is the limit of it.

This first cavity has a luminous spiritual eye that perceives the examination of the realities of the world of sovereignty (malakūt), the wonders of the world of divine science (ʿulūhiyyāt), and all the secrets and subtleties.
