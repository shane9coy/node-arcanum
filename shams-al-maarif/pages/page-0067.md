Chapter: The Description of the Winds
[/Section header]

The first wind is the north wind, which blows from the direction of the pole. The second is sabā (east wind), which comes from the east when the night equals the day. Likewise, below it there is a wind called dabūr. The Arabs hold that dabūr is the one that stirs up and brings the clouds. But sabā arranges them and makes them thick and heavy. There is a wind from the south, which gathers the small pieces of clouds and disperses them. The north wind tears the clouds asunder. In sum: the north wind blows from the north, the south wind from the south, sabā from the east, and dabūr from the west.

[Section header in a decorative rectangular border]
Chapter: The Distances of the Heavens and the Description of the Cloud
[/Section header]

Regarding the forms of the celestial spheres, the teachings of the ancient philosophers have already been related by us. Now we shall describe them in accordance with the people of religious law. In a hadith narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with him, it is related that al-ʿAbbās ibn ʿAbd al-Muttalib, may Allah be pleased with him, stated: We were with the Prophet, the noble and blessed, ﷺ in Baṭḥāʾ. A piece of cloud passed before us. The Messenger ﷺ said: "Do you know what this is?" We said: "It is a cloud." He ﷺ said: "ʿInān is also its name." He ﷺ said: "ʿInān is also this." We said: "ʿInān." Then we fell silent. Then he ﷺ said: "Do you know what the distance is between the heaven and the earth?" We said: "Allah and His Messenger ﷺ know best." He ﷺ said: "It is a distance of five hundred years." The length of each heaven is also of this measure. Upon the seventh heaven there is a river whose depth is also the same. Then above it there is a realm of equal size, and above that is Allah, the Exalted. Nothing of human actions is hidden from Him. The evidence for this matter is the saying of Allah, the Exalted: "Allah is He Who created seven heavens, and of the earth the like thereof." The distance between them from the pace of a human is five hundred years, but the angels traverse this distance in a single moment, and likewise is Satan. His speed is also the same. Abū Wāqid narrated from ʿAlī, may Allah be honored his face. Someone asked him: "What is the distance between heaven and earth?" He said: "The distance of one accepted prayer." Then he was asked: "What is the distance between the east and the west?" He said: "The distance of one day's journey." And someone narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās that the sun moves slightly each year [unclear] visibly.

[Section title underlined]
The Benefits and Advantages of the Sun
[/Section title]

There are many useful purposes and evidences in the sun. One evidence is that the sun is one, and its light illuminates the entire world. Similarly, Allah, the Exalted, is One, "the One," and He governs all the universe. The second evidence is that the sun is very far from us, yet its light is very near. Likewise, Allah, the Exalted, is in His essence far from His creation, yet He is near without direction. The third evidence is that no one is deprived of the sun's light; rather, it is general for all. Similarly, the sustenance of Allah, the Exalted, is not forbidden to anyone and is general for all.

The fourth is that the eclipse of the sun is evidence of the existence of the Day of Judgment. Its setting is darkness. The fifth is that clouds cover the sun—this is the divine knowledge that has been concealed. The benefits of the sun are very many. First: it is the lamp of the entire world. And the saying of Allah, the Exalted: "And We have made the sun an illuminating lamp." Second: it ripens and brings fruit to maturity.
