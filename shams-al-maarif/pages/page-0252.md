[One must] recognize Him with the attributes of beauty, and describe Him with the lights of perfection. His lordship is described toward Him, and one's words are placed before Him within the bonds of servitude. Your glorification is not correct until you purify your soul from every desire, your intellect from the cage of perfection, your attention from worldly desires, your heart from the darknesses of negligence, your body from habits, affectations, consuming the forbidden, and doubts. Then at that time, every Name of the Essence and Attributes will manifest to you.

The master Abraham, upon him be peace, used to narrate special accounts saying: I had removed all desires from my heart; only the desire for pomegranates remained. One day I passed by a man afflicted with a severe illness upon whose entire body ailments had wrapped and consumed his flesh. I greeted that person with salām, and he responded to me with salām and spoke my name, even though I had never before met him. I said in my heart: "This person is from among the friends of God, so let him pray to God Almighty that He deliver him from these ailments." When this thought occurred to me, that person said: "Unlawful intention!" Then, by the light of God Almighty, he prayed and delivered you from the desire for pomegranates, because the ailments' gnawing at the body is better than the gnawing of desires at the heart."

This is the etiquette of sayings. Some of the elders have taught by giving this example. One elder says: A young man met me wearing a single cloak and holding a water sprinkler in his hand. He began to say to me: "I am a person who has intended piety. I eat what people discard, and sometimes it happens that ants come upon it before I pick it up. Should I eat this thing or not? And if I eat it, is there an accounting against me or not?"

This elder says: I began to think in my heart that this person is very pious. Then he looked toward them and found that they were sitting on silver ground. He said to me: "O sincere one, backbiting is forbidden." And they vanished from my sight. These are the people whose eyes God has illuminated and whose remembrance He has purified; in this same way He has illuminated their eyes.

[Heading in a decorative box: Chapter: The Definition and Investigation of Glorification]

Glorification (tasbīḥ) is on the pattern of the form tafaʿīl. It derives from sabbaḥa, which means "to come and go." God Almighty said: "Indeed for you in the day there is prolonged swimming" (sabḥan ṭawīlan). Some say it means "swimming"—that is, traversing the dominions of the malakūt and the subtleties of the divine realities of the jabarūt with swimming. The spiritual traveler swims in the river of the heart with his remembrance, the aspirant swims in the sea of thought with his heart, the lover swims in the sea of longing with his spirit, the gnostic swims in the sea of the unseen with his secret, and the truthful one swims in the sea of the ancient lights with his secret.

One whose power of bestowal is elevated above the meanings of the Names of Attributes, whose states are salted with unveiling, then how great is the commotion from every spiritual traveler due to being veiled from the Presence of the Names! Undoubtedly, one has been ranked by the Name of Majesty, but the lights are not for them until consciousness becomes effaced and the gift of presence occurs. For this reason, the most elevated in witnessing—meaning our master, upon him be peace—says: "Glory be to You; I cannot enumerate praises upon You. You are as You have praised Yourself."

That is: "I cannot praise You; You have praised Yourself." And He said: "O God, increase me in bewilderment by Your lights." And He said: "The Beautiful Names belong to the Name of God, which is not among the two hundred Names of the Essence." Because the Name of the Essence is one that pertains to reality, such that that Name indicates the Name of Reality through some consideration.
