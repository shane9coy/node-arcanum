Its incantation (ʿazīma) is this:
"Answer, O servants of these Names of God. O God, upon the curtains of Your glory, and protect me in the hidden mystery of Your unseen realm, O You who sees but is not seen, and He is over all things Omnipotent."

And the verses are these:
"Deaf, dumb, blind, so they do not return. And We have placed before them a barrier and behind them a barrier and covered them, so they do not see. Upon you both is sent flames of fire and copper, so you cannot obtain victory. O company of jinn and humans, if you are able to pass beyond the horizons of the heavens and the earth, then pass. You will not pass except by authority. So which of the favors of your Lord will you deny? This is a Day upon which they will not speak, nor will they be permitted to apologize. Woe upon that Day to the deniers. Those upon whose hearts God has set a seal."

Chapter: For seeking a beloved one:
When you wish to call someone, take a decayed bone and mix with it the dust from under the feet of the desired one, then knead it with your saliva. Make a thin pillow and insert the heel-bone of a donkey or horse into it, and write the figure of the one desired upon it. Take the garment worn by the desired one and wrap it in it. Make a likeness of the desired one on paper and write the figure of the spirit and the incantation and the desired one and his mother's name inside it, then hang it in the air. The desired one will appear very quickly. And when you enter a realm of tyranny, take a handful of dust and recite this verse: "The gathering will surely be routed and they will turn their backs" along with the incantation, then throw it toward the enemy. The wind of the master will also turn toward them. The effect will occur very quickly and the enemies will flee and be scattered. The incantation is this, and it is also called the "Incantation of Barāhiṭa."

And mention your religious and worldly need, and after it say:
"By the right of this incantation upon you, hasten to what I have commanded you, by the right of the Mighty who takes pride in the might of His might, and fulfill the covenant of God when you have made a covenant."

This supplication is surely accepted.

Now we conclude the book with this firm hope that has reached us from the scholars of the perfected ones and the righteous saints, and it is with the same that Sulaymān concluded his treasures of writings, and this supplication is surely accepted:

"In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful. O God, O You who are the First before every existing thing, O You who are the Last after every perishing thing, O You who were when there was in the heaven no drop and in the earth no tree and for the wind no blowing and for the opening of the clouds no stillness and no sides with the coming of day and the settings of the suns, no [decline]. O You who raised the heaven upon pillars of strength and knew what is beneath it, and spread out the earth upon the cradle of power and knew what is clear upon it, and made the seas flow in the channels of greatness and knew what is beyond them, and sent forth the winds in the horizons of power and knew the settling of their blowing and brought forth..."
