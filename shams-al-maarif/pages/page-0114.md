These are attributes. This is proof that it is the Name of the Divine Essence (Ism al-Dhat). The rest are names of attributes. The Name of the Divine Essence is superior to the names of attributes—this situation is evident. The proof of the correctness of this name is that without it, faith cannot be complete.

His Eminence, the Noble Prophet ﷺ, said: "I have been commanded to fight the people until they say La ilaha illallah. When they say this, their lives and wealth will be safe from me. Thus no other name is sufficient in its place." This is proof that this is the Supreme Name (Ism al-A'zam). It alone saves from Hellfire. His Eminence, the Noble Prophet ﷺ, also said: "Whoever is sincere and testifies with the sincerity of the heart to La ilaha illallah Muhammadun rasulullah, Allah the Most High will make Hellfire unlawful for him." This is the key to Paradise.

Through this same name, one enters Paradise and remains safe from Hellfire. Through it are the pillars of Islam and the excellence of supplication, regarding which sufficient hadith has been mentioned, and the key to prayer is the call to prayer (adhan), which cannot occur without this name. All dhikrs, supplications, and amulets and talismans for healing are arranged upon this Supreme Name. In "Allahumma," the meem has been added because it is the plural of all names. In short, no one is empty of this name. Thus, prayer, which is the pillar of religion—in its opening takbir (takbir al-tahrimah), this is the very name without which, without congregation, prayer cannot be performed. Similarly, in the adhan, iqamah, and the conclusion of prayer, that is "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah," the prayer begins with "Allahu akbar" and concludes with "rahmatullah." This name is at the beginning and end of prayer.

[Heading in a box: Chapter: The Denotation and Requirement of the Supreme Name]

This name is among the names with which Allah the Most High has described His Greatest. I will relate to you an example of it, so that you may know—it is this: A person sometimes knows the name of a medicine whose pleasure and benefits he is also aware of. Then he uses it after this perception. This is the degree of perception of words, that one uses them according to the occasion. Likewise, when a person has known a word and investigated its perfection, the reality is that if he uses it, its fruit will certainly be obtained. Its benefit will be praised. This is the consideration, and words have two states. One is that Allah the Most High makes it flow upon the tongue of a person without him knowing that this is the Supreme Name—so are both sufficient for him, or is one of the two sufficient, or is the other sufficient, and the inquiry is within all of this. Through this, knowledge of the Supreme Name is obtained. Among the degrees, the tongue is most special. Through it, there is greater longing for God's mercy. That through which a servant attains perfection is this: that he becomes acquainted with the reality of this name. If he is not acquainted, good and blessing are still necessary in it. The degrees of perception are also varied. The proof of this is: is a person who has been given understanding and insight by Allah the Most High equal to one who has not? If this name flows upon the tongue of a person upon whom Allah the Most High has not conferred distinction, how can the two be equal? Likewise, consider all degrees. Being acquainted has either this form—this is the Supreme Name—or it is known for infinite good and blessing, or Jabbar, or Jalil, or Jawwad, or Majid, or Majid, or Jami'. Allah the Most High says: "In them are good and beautiful things." Among the names of Allah the Most High is Al-Khabir. Allah the Most High says: "And Allah is knowing of what you do." From here, etymology and the knowledge of arithmetic become evident.

And Allah the Most High says:

"And We have certainly adorned the lowest heaven with lamps."
