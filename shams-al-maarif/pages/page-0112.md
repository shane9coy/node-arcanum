# Page 112 (English translation)
The knowledge of the batin (hidden) names was known through the Greatest Name, but no one except Allah the Exalted knows the complete properties of each name. He bestows upon His prophets and saints as much of it as He wills from His knowledge—which contains effects that no creature has been informed of, not even a messenger prophet or an angel of proximity. The first matter is that when Allah the Exalted desires to honor a servant with this knowledge, He first teaches him ladunni (divinely imparted) knowledge, through which he reaches the station of wilayah (sainthood) and the sciences of the hidden names are opened to him—knowledge that cannot be attained through reasoning or exposition. Then He directs him toward the divine and hidden names. Then, after he has come to know them, He teaches him the things of the world, which refer to the single letters. These are the same letters that appear at the beginnings of the surahs of the Noble Quran.

These are the luminous letters mentioned earlier. After the experts have spoken, understanding of the Greatest Name is granted, with which any supplication is answered and whatever is asked is given. After the experts have spoken, the Greatest Name is learned from al-Khidr (peace be upon him), and sometimes during the descent of mercy upon a servant, the Greatest Name becomes known through divine inspiration. Among the saints of Allah there are various ways of obtaining it. The perfection of the Greatest Name is that for its knower the earth contracts; he can walk upon water and fly through the air, and many karamahs (saintly miracles) appear that are specific to the saints of Allah. Knowledge of these things is not found in books, and they are private between the servant and his Lord. The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said: "The existence of the universe was established first upon the divine names, then upon the effectual names. The divine names are the origin of all things. The affairs of this world and the hereafter are from them. From them all names emerged, and the decree of all matters is determined, which are included in the Mother of the Book." Someone asked Abu Hanifah (may Allah have mercy upon him) about the meaning of "kha-ya-'ayn-sad," and he said: "If I told you its interpretation, you would walk upon water and your feet would not get wet." Sahl ibn Abdullah said: "A man came to Ibrahim ibn Adham and said: 'What do you say about him?' He said: 'In it there is a name such that if a person supplicates with it in purity and righteousness, it is answered.'"

⟨Chapter: The Greatest Name among the Discrete Letters⟩

Each of the discrete letters has meanings. If Allah the Exalted informs a servant about them, karamahs become manifest through him. It is related in the authentic hadith that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said to his companions: "Whenever you meet the enemy, say: 'Hamm la yunsarun.' Hamm is one of the pure names; know it." Sahl ibn Abdullah al-Tustari (may Allah have mercy upon him) says: "The most noble letters from among all the letters of the alphabet are these nine, and all letters are illuminated by their light. They are: alif, lam, ra, qaf, mim, kaf, lam, sad." Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with him) indicated these and their nobility. They are the seven bodies: the seven heavens, the Throne, and the Kursi.

These are the seven bodies intended in the speech of Allah the Exalted: "Alif-lam-mim-sad," "Alif-lam-mim-ra," "Hamm," "Kha-ya-'ayn-sad," and "Ta-sin." These are the fourteen letters that have been called the Greatest Name in both exoteric and esoteric meanings. The Shaykhs of the people of verification, the imams of religion, and the scholars of the spiritual path say that the Greatest Name is from among the divine names, and there is nearly consensus upon this. The interpretation of this name is that it brings things from non-existence into existence. The alif indicates the Eternal Essence, and the saying of Allah the Exalted is: [Noble verse: "Did We not expand for you your breast? And We removed from you your burden, which had weighed upon your back?"] This letter is from among the greatest names because it stands in place of "al-badwa" (the beginning) and "khayr al-a'zam" (the greatest good), and regarding the majesty of the alif, it is that it is paused upon with movement and stillness, through which connection to the divine lights is indicated. The alif includes movement, and there are four types of movement: fatha (vowelization), damma (damma), kasra (kasra), and sukun (stillness). The first of the names of Allah is the name of Essence (Dhat), and the one above movement is attributed to it, because the half of the triple (half of the number three) is connected to it.
