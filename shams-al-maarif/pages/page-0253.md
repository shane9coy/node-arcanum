[Heading inside a decorative box: Chapter: The Division of the Actions of Names]

Know that the actions of names are of two kinds: one is those whose action has been mentioned at the beginning without the name, such as "the wrath of God," "the punishment of God," "the curse of God," and "the bounty of God." The second is those whose mention has come at the beginning, such as "Allah creates what He wills, and Allah is the Creator of all things." And know that the realities of names are of two kinds: one is that for which there is no apparent form that would indicate it to us, and it is to this that the saying of the Prophet, may the peace and blessings of God be upon him, points: "O Allah, I ask You by every name with which You have named Yourself, or sent down in Your Book, or taught to any of Your creation, or chosen in Your knowledge of the unseen." The second kind is that for which we have an apparent form, either symbolic or ritualistic. With us, this is the name that indicates it to us. It also has two subdivisions: one continuous and the other non-continuous. Understand this.

[Heading inside a decorative box: Chapter: The Connection of the Men of the Unseen with the Divine Names]

Know that every person or his other [self] exists in a general or particular way connected to the Divine Names. You should understand this; God Almighty will grant you success. Among the servants of God Almighty are those who are in their own demand, whom they call the "Men of the Unseen," and this is one of the Names of the Divine Essence. Similarly, among these men there is one called the "Ghawth" and "Qutb," and although all the Men of the Unseen take help from the Qutb, they do not know him. And when any name is connected to the Supreme Name in seeking divine help, emotion, and dissolution, that name will be the Supreme Name for that person, and it will do what the Supreme Name does. I have heard from a great mystic who said: For every supplicant there is a specific name appropriate to his prayer, which acts as the Supreme Name does for him—like "the Most Merciful" for Job, peace be upon him; "the Bestower" for Solomon, peace be upon him; and "There is no deity but You, glory be to You, indeed I have been among the wrongdoers" for Jonah, peace be upon him. This is appropriate to the state of the supplicant. This saying is close to the meaning held by the majority of Sufi masters. The mystic Sheikh Muhammad Khwarazmi said in the Sacred Mosque of Makkah in 870 AH: Whoever comes to know the Name of the Divine Essence in his state and speech has attained knowledge of the Supreme Name. Know that God Almighty, by His subtle grace, has manifested various composite names, so that each name indicates its category, nature, and actions, and every seeker may adopt what is appropriate to his rank. Thus the Supreme Name will be in accordance with his purpose, and when he supplicates with this name at its appropriate time with heartfelt attention, it will surely be accepted soon. The Prophet's saying points to this: "In your hearts there are breezes; beware, seek them." By "breezes" is meant the appropriateness of time for seeking and the conformity of the name to the purpose. This is the litany of the disciple that has been revealed to the guardians of the two sacred cities and the close ones.

[Heading inside a decorative box: Chapter: The Secret of Prayer and Action, and the Methods of Works]

Know that in prayer there are comprehensive, predominant, and swift names. The comprehensive ones are those in which the name is without the definite article, such as "the Aware" (Khabir) and "the Subtle" (Latif). Among the people of supplication, the numbers of both of these are multiplied by four at once and throughout most days. The product is taken according to the multiplication. The name should be read with full resolve and with the quality of the inner self; thus they will receive acceptance very quickly. Some of the great ones say that the remembrance of names is the support, the secret, and the hidden knowledge in supplication. By the numerical value of the letters of the names in their formal and ordinal aspects, supplication and humility are combined and the desired result is obtained.
