I have said that which has been stated: that a person's protection from Satan is only through the Basmala. In the Basmala, the word "Ism" (Name) indicates that whatever name follows it is indeed the Greatest Name—that is, Allah—because the Greatest Name is the Name of Majesty, and it is the Pole of all Names, and to it all return, and it is the Great, that is, the famous Name. When someone asks you, "Who is al-Raḥmān?" you will say, "It is Allah." Similarly, all names are attributed to it; from it comes the knowledge of majesty, and its elevated rank is understood. This Name has a great honor above all other names, which is that when you remove the Alif from it, only "Lillah" remains, and when you also remove one Lam, only "Lah" remains, and when you remove this Lam as well, only "Hu" remains. All its letters are self-subsisting. This characteristic is found in no other name, because when you remove one or two letters from any other name, its meaning becomes void. Thus, solely for the reason that no corruption befalls its letters, this Name possesses this honor, and this is proof that the Essence of Allah the Exalted is great, self-subsisting, and eternal. Additionally, this Name has the honor of indicating the singular essence and divine oneness, because the first letter of this Name is Alif, and Alif is the first letter, the first number, and completely solitary. Thus, the Essence of the Most Exalted is also unique in His attribute and singular in His number. This Alif points to this oneness before which all existents bow their heads in submission. At the end of this Name is the Hā', which points to the oneness of divine lordship. This is not found in any name except this one. It declares by its silent tongue: "I am the First, the Last, the Manifest, and I am the Hidden."

Then in the Basmala, after the Name of the Essence comes the attribute of Mercy. Allah the Exalted said: "Say, 'Invoke Allah or invoke the Most Gracious. Whichever one you invoke'" (Quran 17:110). Thus He has given you the choice to invoke Allah or to invoke the Most Gracious, because both the divine lordship and the attribute of mercy are comprehensive attributes. Every name of Allah is great. If you desire the mercy of Allah the Exalted, then invoke "Ya Raḥmān" frequently. The Name "Allah" is the most special among all names, and by consensus, it is the greatest of all names. It is a Syriac name. Its meaning is "the One who brings things from non-existence into existence." It has other meanings as well, which it is better to conceal from the ignorant, so that they cannot commit undesirable actions and motives using this Name of Allah. For example, [they might use it] for falsehood, and may Allah the Exalted protect us all from His wrath. O Allah the Exalted, do not make us among those who seek help from Your Names for sins. This Name contains four letters: one Alif, two Lams, and one Hā'. The natures are also four, and the directions are also four. They are the east, west, north, and south. The proximate angels are also four. They are Jibreel, Mika'eel, Israfeel, and 'Azra'eel. His Eminence Jibreel is the one who delivered prophethood to the prophets. He is the master of conquest and power, through whom Allah the Exalted destroyed the disbelievers.

Israfeel is the master of the Trumpet, who will blow into it three times. The first blow will be of terror, concerning which Allah the Exalted said: "And the day when the Trumpet is blown, whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth will be terrified" (Quran 27:87). And the Trumpet will be blown, so all who are in the heavens and the earth will be terrified, except whom Allah the Exalted wills—none will be terrified. The second blow will be of swooning, which Allah the Exalted described thus: "So whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth will swoon" (Quran 39:68)—meaning all who are in heaven and earth will lose consciousness. The third blow will be of resurrection and standing, which Allah the Exalted Himself described: "Then it will be blown again, and behold, they will be standing, looking on" (Quran 39:68). Thus, each blow has extraordinary characteristics specific to it.

Among the angels, His Eminence Jibreel, peace be upon him, is in charge of all the interests of religion and the world. The overthrow and destruction of the oppressors and the extermination of the disbelievers and the arrogant are connected to him; however, for the believer, he is a source of comfort and mercy and a helper of His allies. Death also comes through him—he sees in it those qualities which Allah the Exalted has created through His grace and mercy for their sake. His Eminence Mika'eel, peace be upon him, is in charge of sustenance for the servants.
