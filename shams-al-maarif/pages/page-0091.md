**Chapter Six: On the Requirements and Properties of Seclusion and Spiritual Retreat**

Know that this occupation is most noble, and through it alone the path to Allah, the Mighty and Glorious, is found. Many saints have performed spiritual retreat in a bathhouse, which is described in the Comprehensive [Book] of Medicine. It is like a grave in its darkness. It is a small chamber where there is no path for light except through the door. When the door is closed it becomes like a grave. When the time for prayer arrives, people would emerge from that chamber; when the imam pronounced the salutation, they would immediately enter it and sit facing the qiblah, looking at no one.

**The Figure of the Tablet of Light and Its Mysteries:**

A certain saint performed spiritual discipline in this very chamber. He often supplicated that the Greatest Name might be revealed to him. One night, while occupied according to his custom with the striving of remembrance and supplication, suddenly a tablet of light appeared before his eyes, in which fifteen lines had been formed. He turned his gaze away from that tablet, thinking that his concentration upon Allah the Exalted might not be distracted. Then the tablet appeared before him again and a voice came saying: "Commit this to firm memory, for this is the thing that will benefit you." Then he opened his eyes and looked at the tablet, and in it there were four lines—one above, one below, and one on the right and left. Between them was a circle. Within it was another circle. In the inner circle was a line that divided it into two parts. Then in the upper half-circle were two lines on both sides, which made it triangular. In the middle of that line was written: "This, rather it is Allah." And at the two angles of the sides was a letter mim.

On the side of the right line, connected to the circle, was written the letter dal, and on the line the name As-Samad was written. Its beginning is from the triangular line and its end is near the circle. On the diameter of the circle was the letter dal. Below the circle was the letter alif, and before As-Samad was the name Al-Wahid [the One]. And on the same line beyond Al-Wahid was the name Al-Qahhar [the Conqueror]. On the second line were the name Ar-Rahman, the name Ar-Rahim, and the name Al-Ghaffar. Inside the triangle, on the diameter, was the letter ta. In the lower half-circle were lines equal to a quarter of the circle. One line ends at the half-circle. Within it was written "rose." Within it, on the opposite side of the circle, was written the letter zay. Within the second quarter of the circle was the letter 5 in Hindi, and outside was written "Samad." In the small circle, which is a quarter of the half-circle, was written "Hu." At the angle where both lines meet at the half of the circle were written "These are ten complete" and the letter waw. Inside the circle the letters were written up to the alif of "There is no deity but He, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer." This mim is opposite it, which is inside the triangle, and the ya of "Sami" is opposite that waw which is at the angle of the circle. The mim of "Qayyum" was opposite the alif. On one side above the circle was written "And Allah is behind them, encompassing." On the other side was written "Rather it is a magnificent Quran in a preserved tablet."
