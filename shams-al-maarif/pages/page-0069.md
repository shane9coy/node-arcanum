# Page 69 (English translation)
This indicates [the direction]. This star is towards the North Pole. Around it are many stars arranged like a fish enclosure/circle.

On one side of them is the star Farqadayn. On the other side is a bright star, and between it and them are three small stars below. They rotate around the Pole. The Pole and Jadha do not move from their places; rather, from the Pole one knows the direction of Jadha, and some say that from Jadha one knows the direction of the Pole. Because in moonlight, except for someone with sharp eyesight, no one can see these stars, and there is only one very faint star among them in their rotation. People test their eyesight with it.

The star Jadha, from which the Qibla is determined, is from Banat al-Na'ash. That is, among the four stars of Banat al-Na'ash, two are Farqadayn which have already been mentioned. One of Banat is Jadha and the other is Suhayl. The method of determining the Qibla from the star Jadha in the kingdoms of Syria and Iraq is to stand with the star behind your back, placing your right ear in alignment. In Egypt, stand with your left ear behind, and your face will be towards the door of the House of God. When you stand with your back towards Farqadayn or Banat al-Na'ash, your face will be towards the Kaaba. Farqadayn are two bright stars near the Pole. Banat al-Na'ash consists of seven stars, of which four form the Bier and three are the Daughters. Similarly, there is Banat al-Na'ash al-Sughra, and the North and South Poles. The Sun and Moon cannot reach them.

Near the South Pole rises the star Suhayl, because this star does not appear anywhere except the Arabian Peninsula, and it rises from the south and sets towards the west. This is a red star. It rises separately from all the stars on the left side of the Qibla and is visible throughout all the lands of Arabia, Iraq, and Syria, but it is not visible in Medina. It rises in Hijaz and somewhat above ten nights [of the lunar month], and appears in Iraq. Some say that Suhayl belongs only to Arabia.

Know that the fixed stars number one thousand or thirty, of which three hundred and twelve stars are in twelve configurations. They lie in the path of the Sun, which are called the Signs of the Zodiac, and three hundred and sixty stars in twenty-one configurations are separate from the Sun's path towards the north, among which are al-Dubb al-Akbar, al-Asfar, and al-Himī, and others. Three hundred and sixteen stars in fifteen configurations are separate from the Sun's path towards the south. No other authority has provided such a detailed account of these stars.

Abu Abd al-Jabbar wrote in his book Basrat al-Kawakib: It is said that among the stars forming the Banat shape is al-Dubb al-Asghar. Its figure is like a vulture sitting with wings spread. It has seven stars, and the Arabs call it Banat al-Na'ash. Four belong to the Bier and three stars are its tail. But there are eight stars outside this configuration. I say that among them is al-Dubb al-Akbar, which has twenty-seven stars, and eight stars are outside its configuration, of which seven are called al-Na'ash. Four belong to the Bier and three to the tail, and among them is the star called Suhayl by the Arabs. And there is al-Himī, which has twenty-one stars. Its shape is like a serpent. Its shape is twisted together with four stars, on whose head is the sign of Scorpio. Among them is al-Likha, which is also called al-Iklil al-Shamali. These stars are situated behind the bright Canopus. One of them is al-Jathi, sitting on its knees. It has twenty-seven stars. One of them is al-Surat, which is also called al-Kura and al-Ijaj al-Rumi, and among them is a turtle, which has ten stars. And there is a very bright star, Nusr Jai, situated there. And one of them is al-Dajaja, which is in the shape of a hen; it has nineteen stars and two stars are outside its configuration. One of them is al-Qit, which has four stars, which lies between the Wise Stars and al-Nasr, above Ra's al-Ghul. Thus these are all the stars of the northern configurations: three hundred and sixty, and the southern configurations have three hundred and twelve stars, among which is Qaytus. It has twenty-two stars. Its figure is like a single animal, with two feet and a tail like a fish's tail. One of them is Jabbar, which has twenty-eight stars; its shape is like a human figure walking with a staff. A belt is tied around its waist and a sword is bound to its waist. There is a very bright star on the sole of its foot, whose name is Rijl [the Foot] Star. Its head is bowed and it is below Rijl al-Jabbar. Its shape is like a rabbit, towards the speech of the south and the direction of the west, above its horn.
