As for why the sun does not have its light blocked by the heavens even though it is in the fourth heaven—the reason is that the heavens are composed of subtle and transparent jewels. Light passes through them. A cloud is a dense substance through which light cannot pass, because it is elevated from the earth and the elements of the earth are mixed within it.

Chapter: On the Moon

It is narrated from Abu Hurairah, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Noble Prophet ﷺ said: "You will see the moon when there are no clouds." The people said: "Yes, indeed." He ﷺ said: "You will see the sun when there are no clouds." Everyone said: "Yes, indeed." He said: "Likewise you will see Allah the Most High." You will say that the Messenger ﷺ gave the example of the sun, even though the sun is more radiant and the moonlight is derived from it. I say there are two reasons for this. The first is that the sun's light overwhelms the eyes; the gaze cannot fix upon the sun, while the eyes see the moon well. Because it turned away from the presence of Allah the Most High, Allah the Most High gave it two things in its place. The incident is as follows: When Gabriel struck the moon with his wing, its light departed, and from that impact its heart broke into pieces. One [gift] is that every month people keep gazing at it, and second, Allah the Most High commanded the Messenger ﷺ to relate such a great and weighty parable. If you say that Allah the Most High said: "The sights cannot comprehend Him, yet He comprehends all sights" meaning Allah the Most High cannot be seen by eyes—and the claim here is not that eyes will see Allah the Most High in such a way that they can encompass Him, because it is impossible for the existence of Allah the Most High to come before us—there are also many benefits in the moon. Among these is that it shines for creation during the night. Second, the miracle of our Noble Prophet ﷺ was manifested through it. The Exalted One has said: "The Hour has drawn near and the moon has been split," meaning the Day of Judgment has approached and the moon has been broken apart. Allah the Most High appointed stations for it so that time may be known; by its light the darkness is diminished, as the Exalted One has said: "We have effaced the sign of the night." If this were not the case, people would remain occupied in their affairs even at night, and there would be no distinction between night and day. Ali, may Allah honor his face, said that sleeping naked in moonlight causes leprosy, and when garments are washed and spread out in moonlight, their color changes.

Chapter: On how long the Moon remains in each of its stations each night, and their names are these: Shartayn, Bateen, Thurayya, Dabar, Haq'ah, Na'ah, Dhir'a', Nathrah, Turfah, Jabhah, Zibhrah, Sarfah, 'Awa', Simak, Ghafar, Zabania, Iklil, Qalb, Shulah, Nu'aym, Baladah, Sa'd al-Dhabih, Sa'd Bul', [watermark: Sa'd al-Sa'ud Sa'd al-Akhbiyah] Muqaddam Mu'akhkhar, Rashaf.

Chapter: On the Signs and Remedies of the Planets and Stars

Allah the Most High says:
"And it is He who has made for you the stars, that you may be guided by them in the darknesses of the land and the sea."
And everywhere it has passed: "And by the star they are guided."

It is narrated from Ibn Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said: The knowledge of all of these is the knowledge of stars, which is a great science. Most people fall short of it. In this speech there is an allusion to the knowledge of astrology, not to those [practices] upon which reliance has been placed. There is in it the possibility of this misconception: that the moonlight is derived from the sun's light. The known planets are seven; among them is the pole, from which the pole star is known and the direction of the qiblah [text obscured by watermark: is determined].
