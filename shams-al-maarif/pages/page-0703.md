Chapter: The Virtues of the Name "Al-Matin" (The Firm/Strong)
[decorative header border]

This name is applied to He upon whom a name is bestowed, because steadfastness and firmness pertain to bodies, while God Almighty is exempt from them. Its meaning indicates power and dominion, and steadfastness indicates the intensity of power. God alone fulfills His power and possesses complete strength. Since He is the All-Powerful, the Possessor of power and dominion, therefore the meanings of "Matin" are most appropriate. The spiritual retreat (khalwa) for this name is with lawful sustenance. If one recites the name "Al-Qayyum" joined with it, the desired result will descend. This name belongs to the realms of Jibreel (Gabriel).

The reciter of this name is granted two mantles (gifts). If he gazes upon a sinful person, that person will repent; all his needs will be fulfilled:

He will grant you two mantles and fulfill your needs, and when you gaze upon the sinful person, he will immediately repent, and he will become an unveil-er of very wondrous things. Its talismanic figure (naqsh) is as follows:

Any child who performs ritual ablution (ghusl) with this name's first letter—let that person write it and keep it with himself—if someone's strength has been diminishing due to some illness. Also write the name of "Mojrikal" around the talisman. Then observe its effect in the spiritual retreat.

For children to walk:

If any child is unable to walk or move about, tie the talisman of this name around his neck; he will soon begin to walk and move about. Likewise, for a traveler, keeping it with him during the journey will prevent the hardships of travel. Its mention has passed in the name "Al-Qawi" (The Strong).

Chapter: The Virtues of the Name "Wali" (The Protector/Friend)
[decorative header border]

"Wali" is he who takes charge of the affairs of servants—that is, the beloved and the one who grants forgiveness to his allies. God Almighty says:

ذٰلِكَ بِاَنَّ اللّٰهَ مَوْلَى الَّذِيْنَ اٰمَنُوْا وَ اَنَّ الْكٰفِرِيْنَ لَا مَوْلٰى لَهُمْ ۝

Translation: "This is because God is the Protector of those who believe, while the disbelievers have no protector."

That is, God is the solver of problems and helper of the believers, while the disbelievers have no solver of problems. The meanings of "wali" are also related to "nearness." He says: "So woe to you, and woe" (refers to proximity). They also call rain "wali" when it comes abundantly, because life arrives with its growth. And the person who remains after the dead is also called "wali" (the survivor). They also call the ruler "wali" and "wali" (governor).

"He sends down rain after they have lost hope, and spreads His mercy" (refers to God's mercy as "wali" for the believers).
