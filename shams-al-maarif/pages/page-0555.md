# Page 555 (English translation)
Chapter 36: On the Divine Overflow, the Radiant Light, the Sacred Stone, and the Secrets and Symbols of Plants

In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful

Praise be to God for those blessings by whose bestowal He granted us knowledge of the inner truth and opened for us the gates of wisdom, and the curtains of ignorance's darkness were removed from us, and He gave us excellence over much of His creation through His blessings, and upon our master Muhammad, who is the guide upon the illuminated path. May the generous God send down upon him such blessings as will always precede him in his service and enter upon him in the Garden of Eden.

O God! Grant me and us guidance upon the straight path. Since I first learned of this art of divine craft, I have always gazed toward this art and researched, reflected upon, and contemplated its nuances and subtleties until finally I began to swim in this surging sea and wrapped myself in its luminous shroud, and became the master of the keys of this treasure. Whatever came to pass was through divine warning.

It became known to me that when the Exalted Lord favored me with this wisdom, it was revealed to me that I should also present some portion of this knowledge in my book, so that the rust might be removed from the hearts of the ignorant and this treasure might endure after me. For in this age this knowledge has been completely obliterated—neither do its knowers appear, nor is any news of them to be had. Jabir says: "Great is the regret concerning this knowledge, for no one knows it nor derives benefit from it." I therefore studied many books of this art and observed many modes, behaviors, and statements of these people, and I studied their books. The author of the hermitic craft is the philosopher Pythagoras, and the philosopher Hiraẓ, and the composition of nearly two hundred books is attributed to Jabir ibn Ḥayyan, and the philosopher Abū Bakr al-Rāzī, and the treatises of the philosopher Hermes, and the books of Hippocrates and Hermes and Galen, and the books of ʿArus and Rudia, and Kunū Tankīn, and among the sages Maria and Astur—the philosopher Mukhallad ibn Yazīd, etc. For some twelve years I toiled among the books of the philosophers and searched for this wisdom in every city and village, as Mukhallad said:

Verse:

"I shall not abandon my quest, nor care
What I endure from exile and want,
Perhaps my time will favor me, and I shall prosper,
Or sorrow and pain shall depart from me."

Translation:
I shall not desist from my purpose, nor shall I be concerned about the hardships I bear from the fatigue of travel and poverty—perhaps my age will assist me, so I shall succeed; far from me shall be sorrow and grief.

Until finally God the Exalted opened it upon me, together with those devices and operations which I had derived through my own intellect and invented through my own mind. For previously I would discover the means for half of an operation, but the second half of the operation would not be attained, nor would any knower of it be found, and whatever I attempted would all go awry. Until finally the correct method became known to me and the truth of the philosophers' statements was revealed to me. I pray to God that He forgive my sins, for I have revealed the working of a very great matter. And I seek refuge with God lest any incompetent person gain entry into this book of mine, so that no benefit reaches anyone—for He is the forgiving and the merciful. (Continued on page 554)
