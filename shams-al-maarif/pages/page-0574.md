# Page 574 (English translation)
The Craft of One of My Western Friend (Gold and Silver Recipe):

He says that if the natrun (black salt), red powder, and an equal weight of white marble—some also call it 'ali—be taken, and all of them finely powdered and sealed in an earthenware pot, and left in a furnace for two days and two nights. Then remove them and add four parts more water and boil it over fire, and gradually add these medicines to it, and continue boiling the water until one-third has evaporated. At that time, remove the fire and decant the water and store it safely. When needed, place it in earthenware vessels and keep it over this gentle fire, as with a lamp flame, and let the aforementioned water drip drop by drop onto quicksilver. After a short while, increase the fire gradually from mild to double strength, and continue until it combines with the water. Then increase it further to double again and maintain the heat. Continue until the quicksilver becomes well fixed. When you have succeeded in this manner, two treasures have come into your possession—both white and red—for with every base substance this same key is required. Some perform this operation by adding three uqiyyas of sulfur to the heated water, or less, when the water amounts to one ratl. The sulfur immediately turns black like chalk, and three uqiyyas of iron filings are also added, which dissolve as well. Then bring it to a vigorous boil until it reaches a consistency like cream, and a bright yellow color resembling saffron will appear on its surface. Carefully skim off this layer with cloth, and while the mixture remains hot, plunge it twenty-one times into the glowing coals of a crow. Next, combine one uqiyya of the crow preparation with one uqiyya of lunar quicksilver, grind them together thoroughly, and the result will be a silver-like substance that displays the appearance of gold.

Method of Stabilizing Quicksilver in alchemical fire (The Nature of Rujr Laj):

Take sixty mithqals of wood from the margī tree and shape it, then blend in two ratls of qatr flower oil and crush thoroughly. Seal this mixture in a glass vessel with clay, and for the base metals prepare one ratl of sulfur mixed with marg stone kasīs and one part of rubā, dissolving together and heating in fire throughout the night. Then dissolve one part of sulfur in the fire. Continue this process until all parts are dissolved and the quicksilver turns red. After this, wash the sixty mithqals of quicksilver with water and rape seed, dissolve it with egg white, rinse with warm water, and place the resulting powder beneath and above the quicksilver. Form a sheet of mithqal-sized gold, seal the bowl with clay science, and set it over fire. When the water dries, add more water until the quicksilver ceases all sensation and motion. Then heat it in an earthenware pot and purify it with salt and nushādir—after this it will never change.

A Golden Recipe That Has Reached Me from Some Scholars:

Study this carefully and commit it to memory. The preparation is as follows: Take fine sīs (litharge), clean it thoroughly, and fill a clay vessel with salt water or urine, placing the litharge inside so that it is thoroughly moistened. Seal the vessel and calcine it in a gentle fire overnight until the sīs turns red from the vinegar's effect. Then separately grind one part of nushādir and an equal amount of sulfur, mix and grind them together, and combine this red litharge with these medicines, performing sublimation three times until the nushādir and sulfur become red. At this point, combine one part gold with three parts quicksilver fixed by the Rujr Laj method described above, or if that is unavailable, use quicksilver that has been transmuted through litharge. From this gold-mercury mixture, make balls with the sulfur-nushādir preparation, grind everything very finely for more than a day, then perform sublimation in a well-sealed glass vessel, repeating the process until the quicksilver and gold become thoroughly red. At this stage, one part of this medicine will transmute silver into gold, and upon testing it proves completely reliable.

A Wonderful Recipe of Mā' al-Ra's (Water of the Head):

Take one part of bitter salt and an equal weight of powdered rape seed, along with one māshā of marw, mix together, and wash thoroughly in qwār gundal.
