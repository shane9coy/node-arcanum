# Page 242 (English translation)
The Reality of Divine Unity is the mean perfection, from which possible things are incapable at its beginning and before reaching its irradiation of lights are helpless. His pure essence is. How lofty is His glory. His title is "the Sufficient One" and "the Hand of Power." He alone is the Essence toward whom the utmost of needs is directed; from Him success in desires is attained.

The praise from the perspective of speech and vision, from the highest to the highest, is not even possible for His essence. The knowledge of His essence is impossible for human understanding. He is the cause of those who attribute [qualities], or He is the attribute. The grandeur of Allah is not possible without human knowledge of the path, because He Himself is its knower. For this reason He did not mention this quiddity and contented Himself with its concomitants. Thus we say: for the First Principle, nothing is associated with attributes; He is pure unity, purified from the multitude of all existents. And these are the concomitants of these causes. When you mention them with proximate concomitants, omitting the remote concomitants of His explanation, then the requirements of His haqiqah will be nullified—because if attributes are with Him, they are not necessary for His essence, and His existence depends on requirements, and this does not unify except when unity is such that it has neither extremity nor division. For the application of "one" to an essence occurs when the application of an attribute is made along with it, and the application of "the One" occurs when no consideration of attribute is made. And whatever essence possesses haqiqah, necessarily it will be obtained through the gathering of certain parts, and that essence's haqiqah will depend upon the presence of those parts—so such an essence is in reality not self-subsisting, to which the name "the One" refers.

His word has two meanings: one calls "samad" that thing which has no hollow, and second, "samad" means "lord." Both meanings apply to the Divine Essence, because whatever possesses quiddity will necessarily have a hollow and an interior; this is its significance, and its interior is its self. He is the existent, He is the deity. According to the second [meaning], its meaning is relational, meaning the Divine Essence is the complete origin, and it may also be that all things are in need of Him while He is in need of no one. "He begetteth not, nor was He begotten" explained that all are dependent upon Him; He alone is the one who grants existence to all, and He alone is the generous toward them. It then became necessary to state that the birth of anything from Him is impossible, because every essence of such a kind will necessarily have its quiddity shared between it and its other—for a person does not exist without a intermediary or relation.

The "ayn" and "qadar" will be the same thing, or will have some relation to matter, from which birth proceeds, and Allah the Most High is purified from this. The summary of the discourse is: since His haqiqah is not borrowed from His other, therefore He is independent of it, and He exists self-subsisting by His very essence. He is the Knowing of secrets and the Reckoner—this is for those people whose mention has come in the Quran. The attribute "Samad" is what initiates and completes His reference perfectly. And the interpretation of "There is none comparable to Him" is that in the realm of existence, no one is His equal—because if someone were equal to Him in the realm of existence, then necessarily their quiddity would be shared between them and their other, and that [being] would be born from its other, so that Allah the Most High is purified from these things.

Chapter: The Preference of La ilaha illa Allah over All Words

The rank of divine unity has become manifest over all chapters and verses. I clarify the reality of oneness: "La ilaha illa Allah"—rather, "La ilaha illa Hu"—that "Hu" refers to the Oneness, and it is not opened for anyone other than Him. The eye of any seer, the light of understanding, cannot reach this. Every secret cannot be revealed, nor can every excellence be desired. And revealing the secret of haqiqah is kufr. When we said that revealing the secret of the batin is kufr, then revealing the secret of reality becomes a great kufr. Let it not remain hidden that kufr has various pillars, and the tacit allusion pointed toward all its believers, that the disbelievers are seekers of the Greatest Name, which only the intimate ones of Allah know.
