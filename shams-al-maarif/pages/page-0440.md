**The Practice of Obtaining Proximity to Truth and the Acceptance of Prayer**

The Ism al-Samīʿ holds great proximity to God the Exalted. Its client is named Thabāʾīl, under whom are four officers, each of whom rules over 180 ranks, and each rank contains 180 clients. The prayer of Ism al-Samīʿ is this:

O You who hear, You are the one who hears righteousness and piety, and You are the one who knows wisdom and piety, and You are the one who appears in the hearts of Your lovers with the secret of manifestation, and You are the one who knows what is more subtle and more hidden, and You see without "where"; You do not sleep, and the creeping of the black ant upon the bare rock in the night of darkness beneath the layers of dust is not hidden from You. I ask You by the subtleties that You have inserted into hearing and sight, and by the finest details that You have written in victory to benefit the place of hearing, and by the merits of what You have concealed in hearing that the place of hearing may stand in the position of victory, that You grant me secrets enrolled within the encompassment of sight, and the witnessing of lights determined near the encompassment of sight by hearing, and grant me by the light of Your sign and the casting of the secret of Your trust, and the permanence of observation of what You will upon Your highest footstep, and Your encompassing perception of the compendiums of healing, and aid me upon understanding the demands of the soul by the turning of accounting. Indeed, You are the collector of every good and the repeller of every evil. O Lord of the worlds.

**The Practice of Hidden Things and Knowing the Secrets of Hearts:**

The special properties of Ism al-Baṣīr (The Seeing Name) of God the Exalted are that whoever wishes to know the secrets and states of people's hearts should perform the dhikr of this Name. Its client is named Ḥarfiyāʾīl. He rules over four officers, each of whom oversees 303 ranks, and each rank contains 303 clients. The client comes to the dhākir of this Name and tells him two qualities—one apparent and one hidden. The apparent quality is that when he acts, he causes the observer to look upon the reality of the people of nobility. The hidden quality is that hidden matters become known to him, and the client always remains with him. The prayer of Ism al-Baṣīr is this:

O You who see, You are the one who helps the hidden secret of hidden things and the inner thoughts, and You perceive the sensory experiences of the secrets of the people of insight, and the witnessing of the delicate inner matters flowing in thoughts. I ask You by the springs of the light of Your essence, and the emergence of the perceptions of Your insights, and the unveiling of the meanings of Your sight and Your magnitudes, that You make me one who sees every hidden thing, and grant me a content eye with the light of oneness and existence, so that I may perceive the secret of Your uniqueness in the station of proximity. O You who, through You, is with You at the unveiling of the secret: There is no god but You. You are what You will.
