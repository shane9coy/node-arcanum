# Page 665 (English translation)
[Heading: Chapter: The Virtues of the Name "Razzāq" (The Provider)]

The Razzāq is essentially He who created both the provider (razzāq) and the one provided for (marzūq) and granted them the means of sustenance.

There are types of sustenance. God Himself does not eat; He feeds everyone:

Sustenance is of two kinds: apparent and hidden. The strength of bodies is through the hardship of the intellect, equal to the balance of the response and its causes for the stability of the response—and this belongs to the hidden realm of bodies, because of that which stands beside it, the side of refuge. But in relation to the one who feeds, its station is separate, and the intention is long-standing. He does not do this except by the truth of Glorious and Exalted He, because He feeds and does not Himself eat, and this attribute belongs to His place and not to anyone else. Because when the Exalted God brought forth all things and created the intellect as a luminous creation, for it is the first addressee—the first rank and the first manifestation. Thus the mystery of that ancient address was revealed. Then with it the address manifests and it does not become separate from it; rather, its Word shall not be annihilated. All of His lordship is upon it, and the constant non-existence of the power of hearing is also mercy for creation, for they are veiled between the layers of composition. Thus His Word is not veiled from them, for upon them the aid of composition is obtained by choosing sensations, sweetness, and abandoning comfort—and this is the sustenance of the intellect. The second sustenance is that of the spirit. That is, when the Exalted God brought forth souls from form and established them wholly with them.

Thus the matter is like the manifestation of a great intellect, and this is from the world of the Command, and this is the aforementioned Divine Word. It is in the capacity of command upon the heart, whose refuge is very distant, and this world persists with it until it is established, and such is the state of the Command. It is with every soul and tongue. The third sustenance is that of the souls, and it is the head of the dispensation in the world of reality, with that same mystery which the Exalted Lord has placed within it—

[A handwritten scribble/strike-through crosses the words "in the world of reality, with that same mystery"]

—the secrets of the subtleties of worlds and existents.

[Heading: Sublime Souls Are Mirrors for Terrestrial Forms:]

And this soul is the mirror of sublime and terrestrial forms, and every form creates for it a reality that is its nourishment.

And the fourth sustenance is that of composition, and it is that the heart of the whole is with the dispensation of the letters of composition of the giver, which are established. They emanate from the soul and descend upon the spirit with the intellect so that the result may manifest, and the lights of the varieties of letters become great, and they are the temple for the lights of the elements.

The Exalted God says:

"Verily, by the remembrance of God hearts find tranquility." Ṭāʾ

Translation: "Hearts find peace only through the remembrance of God."

[Heading: Hidden Sustenance Is Unlimited, Apparent Sustenance Is Limited:]

Thus the hidden sustenance always remains, like the reality of the spirit, and the sustenance of the apparent is limited, and inspiration is its encounter. God has gathered both categories in one place and mentioned the sustenance of the sublime and the terrestrial. He says:

"Is there any creator other than God who provides you sustenance from heaven and earth?"

Translation: "Is there any creator besides God who provides you sustenance from heaven and earth?"

Thus its original sustenance is for the people of the hidden realm and the angelic spirits, and bodily sustenance is for the people of bodies—and these are the people
