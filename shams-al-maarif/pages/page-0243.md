He has said so and has maintained His own existence; there was no contradiction in reason and the infidelity of love. Understand this strange and wondrous sign. The second matter is that in which lies the exit from separation of love and from unveiling—there are those traces of footsteps and of obligation within the passes of the mysteries of divine mercy. They are secrets for the people of divine unity and the people of allusion, but in the primal origins, the sweetness of verification flows forth, and from the valley of spirits, springs of springs gush forth. From the first valley, one of the riders of those brought near, and from the second valley, one is the wing—Prophet Khiḍr, peace be upon him. The first spring belongs to annihilation, and the second to subsistence. For the second, there is the sign of wisdom. So the first is Jerusalem, and the second is pure unitariness—that is, "Indeed, I am Allah; there is no deity but Me; so worship Me." And regarding the saying of Allah the Most High: "Has there come to you the story of Moses, when he saw a fire"—He informs about it, and looking from it was established. Then He placed a veil over that fire without words. Then He said to Moses: "Indeed, I am Allah; there is no deity but Me; so worship Me." The means of union was established as divine unity, and the limit of "I said" was appointed together with obedience. I shall explain to you a rare sign: it is this—if you are told that you are not in the station of Moses, then at that time you will not feel the pleasure of that address, nor will the sweetness of union come to you. Someone asked Prophet Moses, upon him be peace: "How did you know that this address was from Allah?" He said: "The pleasure of the call killed me, and I became unaware of my own being. Every part of my body and every hair gained its sweetness from it, because the address came from the call, and from all directions, silence and awe and the Ahmadīyah came over me. Then I understood that this address was from Allah the Most High. I said: 'You are that essence which will always remain, and You alone are He before whom there is no station or movement of speech for Moses.' Reflect upon these utterances, then you and your form and your desire are yours." In this same manner, the presence of the Noble Prophet, may Allah bless him and grant him peace, informed that Allah the Most High says: "O My servant, I became ill and you did not visit Me. In both worlds, I am the only God." The beloved and pleasing servant is he—when you become ill, he visits you, and when you ask for forgiveness, he forgives you. The summary of this sign is that you become completely unaware of your own self and attributes; make your heart its dwelling place; make your existence Mecca and your consciousness the Sacred Mosque, and circumambulate around it—you will find Allah the Most High, not the house of the Ka'bah. That which is like the secret of life is the Living, the Self-Subsisting, the Intense in Existence. That which is the secret of the formation of creation and of states—this is a proof upon the oneness of Allah the Most High's power from the origins of light and wisdom.

Chapter: The Unparalleled Properties of the Holy Quran

The saying of Allah the Most High is: "Allah bears witness that there is no deity but He, and the angels and those having knowledge, maintaining justice." This verse has three meanings. The first is that Allah the Most High, in pre-eternity, Himself affirmed His necessary existence and illuminated those attributes, and together with this, the companionship of others is precluded. The second is a glance toward the angels, who verified His existence in their state of being; this witness is with evidence and constitutes knowledge of their character; there is no return to it, for they are free from dryness of defects and from predominance of form. The third is the good tidings with which Allah the Most High has kept His servants firm, praising them with knowledge of justice and verification. Ibn 'Abbās, may Allah be pleased with him, states: "The sublimity of speech is that Allah the Most High bore witness to His own essence, even if no one else bore witness to Him, that the true deity is He—there is no other besides Him. The angels also bore this witness. The prophets and believers, who are people of knowledge, bore witness, and they are established with justice, because they are people of justice. The meaning of justice is placing each thing in its proper place, and this comes only from knowledge. There is no deity besides Him; He is the Mighty, the Wise. He remembers them with love, which humanity cannot abandon, and He is wise with those upon whom He has placed obligatory duties, and with those who sincerely bore witness from their hearts to the deity of Allah—bearing witness that surely with Allah, the religion of Islam is the true religion.
