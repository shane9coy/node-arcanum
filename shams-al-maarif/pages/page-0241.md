Chapter 15

Necessary Conditions of Operations and Properties of the Name al-Quddus (The Holy)

The first section discusses the conditions that are necessary for certain operations at their beginning or end. May God Almighty grant us and you the success to understand His obedience and the secrets of His names, for God Almighty has created angels who bear the Throne and the Chair, and they act by His command and are assigned to the Tablet. For each of them, God—glorious and exalted—has appointed specific dhikrs and forms of worship. Likewise, the dhikrs for the people of heaven are separate. Thus, the dhikr of the angels who are in the Supreme Assembly is "Quddus." The dhikr of the people of the Chair is "Subḥān Allāhi Rabbi al-Malā'ikati wa al-Rūḥ" (Hallowed be God, Lord of the Angels and the Spirit).

Know that the Name al-Quddus (The Holy) manifests meanings upon the one who traverses the subtle mysteries of the Divine Power (Jabarut) and mentions it. This degree of Supreme Divine Power establishes the conclusive truths regarding the non-occurrence of compositional generation and the ultimate limits of realities. This is the Supreme Divine Power (Jabarut A'la) whose luminous lights of elevation and subtlety have become manifest.

The properties of the Name al-Quddus are that if one combines it with the Name Subḥ (Hallowed), meaning if one mentions "Subḥān Quddus," then eight things become revealed to him: the Supreme Dominion (Malakut A'la), the Throne ('Arsh), the Chair (Kursi), the Tablet (Lawḥ), the Pen (Qalam), the Supreme Assembly (Mala' A'la), the Station (Al-Mustawā), and the Clouds (Al-Ghammām).

The Master of the Noble Prophet, upon him be peace, said: "I reached the Station of Al-Mustawā, and I heard the sound of pens that occurs during writing." Likewise, the property of "Subḥān Allāhi Rabbi al-Malā'ikati wa al-Rūḥ" is that the dominion of Divine Power, the kingdom, and the Supreme Dominion become manifest. There are eight things within it: the structure (Imārat), moisture (Ruṭūbat), coldness (Barūdat), dryness (Yubs), dominion (Malakūt), life (Ḥayāt), the eternal explanation (Tabyīn Abad), and eternal subsistence (Baqā' Abad).

This is the dhikr of the Bearers of the Throne and the Holy Spirit, peace be upon him. They are magnificent angels whom God Almighty has not created from any creature. They are possessed of divine inspiration. Some say they are the same as Gabriel, peace be upon him, who is the reality of descent and revelation. God Almighty said: "The Trustworthy Spirit descended with it upon your heart" — meaning the Holy Spirit descended with the Quran upon your heart. These are the eight chief angels of the people of the Supreme Assembly.

Purification is the collection of the lights of sanctity, for the Holy Spirit dwells in the Holy Presence (Quds) and manifests in pure hearts along with the realities of faith. It is the very divine revelation, and it is near the Lote Tree of the Extremity (Sidrat al-Muntahā) before the Powerful One (Ḥaḍrat al-Qadīr). Quds is that which is pure from defects and imperfections, and it is immersed in Its attribute of perfection, just as glory and unity are deficient in themselves.

Know that the true light of tawḥīd (divine unity) and the pure source are in the Chapter of Sincerity (Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ), and the verses that correspond to it are within it. For this reason, the Chapter of Sincerity is called "One-Third of the Quran," for only three things remained in the noble Quran: ordinances and tawḥīd. Thus, we derive its explanation and the esoteric meaning through thought and intellect, and we state its meanings and the great matters with all brevity. It has relation only with God, and His saying is: "Say: He is God, One" — He is the One who exists by His essence, the Necessary Existent, the very essence beside which there is no deity. He is by His essence, He is with His own self, there is no other besides Him. Thus, absolute oneness and the particularity of the Name are its meanings; this is the secret of divinity, for the source is that to which all are attributed, while It cannot be attributed to anything else.

Thus, the absolute is that which encompasses all comparison, and Its other is attributed to It. Divinity is indeed that very divinity: to express It along with its necessary attributes is the ultimate perfection.
