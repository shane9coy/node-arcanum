Manzil Simāk: In the manzil Simāk there are two stars, one called Simāk al-A'ẓal and the other called Simāk Rāmiḥ. Some call both of them the shanks of the Leo constellation, and some call them its legs and shins. In front of Simāk Rāmiḥ there is a star that is its spear. In front of Simāk al-A'ẓal there is no star, and for this reason it is called al-A'ẓal. Its figure is this: *. This manzil is called Simāk because it resembles a fish in the sky. Behind Rāmiḥ there is a star called 'Ajz al-Asad.

Manzil Dhābiḥ: In the manzil Dhābiḥ there are two stars, one at a hand's distance apart, and near both of them there is a small star as if it is slaughtering it; for this reason it is called Dhābiḥ. Its figure is this: ✴

Manzil Sa'd al-Su'ūd: It is one star, as if its mouth is open wanting to swallow something. Its figure is this: [Manzil Sa'd al-Su'ūd symbol: an eight-pointed star surrounded by a circle of twelve dots]

Manzil Sa'd al-Khabī'ah: In it there are three stars but they appear as one star. Between them there is a fourth star. Its figure is this: ✴ ✴ ✴ Some count nine stars in it. Some say the first star is Sa'd al-Su'ūd, the second is Sa'd al-Dhāri', the third is Sa'd al-Akhbiyah, and the fourth is Sa'd Bul'. It has four stars. The Moon enters it one day in the year. Among the lunar mansions there are four called Sa'd: Sa'd al-Khābi'ah, Sa'd al-Su'ūd, Sa'd al-Dhābiḥ, and Sa'd Bul'. Each of them has two stars. They appear at a hand's distance from each other.

Manzil Farq al-Dalw Muqaddam and Mu'akhkhar: Each of them has two stars. The first is Muqaddam and the second is Mu'akhkhar, as if both are pouring. The word Farq means bringing water in the bucket, therefore both are called Farq.
