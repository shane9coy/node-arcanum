# Page 732 (English translation)
By the excellence of turning with sincere devotion towards You, may the descents upon the discourse befall me, and may Your care attach itself to me at all times, O Allah, O Most Mighty, O Pure, O Hidden, O Manifest, O Possessor of Glory and Honor, O Possessor of Grace, O Zajanam

Whoever recites this dhikr constantly—Allah, the Exalted, makes him self-sufficient from creation and grants him complete contentment.

Chapter: The Virtues of the Name "Malik al-Mulk Dhu al-Jalal wa al-Ikram" (The King of Kings, Possessor of Glory and Honor)

Malik al-Mulk is He who disposes of His creation as He wills—whether He brings someone into existence or annihilates him. The meaning of mulk (kingdom) is malik (owner), and the malik is one who possesses complete power. All existing beings are His subjects; He is the Owner and Disposer of all, and all existing things constitute a single dominion, because some of them are connected to others—like man, who is one reality while his limbs are diverse, yet all these limbs share in the governance of one existence. Similarly, the parts of the world are like the limbs of a human being; they are one in the sense of being delimited, and this is the totality of the world to which the Divine Existence gives being. Due to its being organized in this manner—its parts being connected by the bond of oneness that belonged to the ownership of God, and Allah was its Owner—every servant's dominion is particularly established in his body. When His will takes effect in the faculty of willpower and desire within it, that one becomes Malik al-Mulk according to his divine power.

The Glory of Allah is an attribute of His Essence, while Honor is an attribute of His actions:

Glory is His essential attribute, and Generosity is an attribute of action, because for generosity to take place, creation must exist upon which He may bestow generosity. However, Dhu al-Jalal wa al-Ikram is particular to the human world together with its honor. Allah, the Exalted, says: "We have honored the children of Adam" (Quran 17:70). His blessing and honor are bestowed indiscriminately upon the believer, the disbeliever, and the disobedient—this has already been explained in the section on the Great Name, and we do not wish to extend it here. The meaning of honor is generosity—meaning that His gifts, blessings, and bounties flow upon every obedient person, sinner, believer, and disbeliever alike. This is what He says: "We have honored the children of Adam." This generosity of His extends over all humanity, but the generosity particular to the believers is this: He has established the believer in His service, taught him the means of His power, made him acquainted with the realities of the ranks, and promised them through the tongue of His Prophet. And this is His generosity—how He has dealt with the people of Paradise. His generosity in this world is that the heart remains connected to Him through the Names, and the blessing of the Hereafter is that he attains the complete form of the parts of action.

The vision of the Lord will occur in Paradise, not in this world:

Glory belongs to Him who rules over all existences, and because of which His vision is impossible in this world. It will occur on the Day of Resurrection in the gathering place, and the lights of that vision come to the one who sees with illumination, and from them and their lights, one power and one [vision] is produced, by which seeing with other visions will be granted. This is their vision of reality.

[One] could not endure the radiance of the light of the face of Moses, peace be upon him; among the Children of Israel, some turned aside to worship the calf, while others covered their faces out of shame.
