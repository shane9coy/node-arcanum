**The Prophet ﷺ gave a detailed account of the creation of the world**

When ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn asked the Prophet ﷺ about the moon and the heavens and the earth, he said: Allah was, and nothing was with Him, nor was there anything before Him. He is the First of all, and there is no first before Him, and He is the Last of all, and there is no last after Him. The first thing that Allah created is the Pen. Then after that He created the Tablet, and He said to the Pen: "Write." The Pen said: "What shall I write?" He said: "Whatever is going to happen until the Day of Judgment." Write everything. So it wrote. Then He created the Throne, then He brought into existence the particles of beings, and He encompassed them with the Pen, and He numbered them with the Word. Despite their differing components, He then spread over them nature through His Will and the governance of His Wisdom. Then He manifested the intellects upon the oneness that was destined for them, then He created the spirits in the form of commands. Then He created the breasts and made them the center of spirits and the resting place of life, then He created the malakūt on high. Then He created the letters from the lights of greatness and deposited them in the Preserved Tablet, in which remembrance is written—not the Pen, never, and not from Pen's division, but rather it is only the eternal Will directed toward the divine command.

وَلَقَدْ كَتَبْنَا فِي الزَّبُورِ مِنْ بَعْدِ الذِّكْرِ أَنَّ الْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحُونَ ۝
Translation: "Indeed, in the Psalm after the Remembrance, We have written that the earth shall be inherited by My righteous servants."

**He created 12 commands for the establishment of the world; all these creations are from these 12 commands. Each command is described in detail:**

Then He created the world of malakūt. When He organized these multiple worlds of names and the divine registries of creation, and manifested through His twelvefold command by which the world is established—through it the world of ḥulūl lawhūt [divine manifestation] became twelvefold, and commands originated from it. The first command is that which was with the act of creation. It was obligatory upon both the receiving and the refusing, upon all intentions and intellects on the Day of Seizing. There was nothing but Allah until the Day. Within the sight is gathered the act of the trust and its conveyance. And the second command is that with which the Throne was established, so that all inhabitants of heaven and earth may attain perfection. And the third command is that with which the Chair [Chair of Divinity] is established, and the schematic forms of all beings of the cosmos are upon it in summary form. The fourth command is that with which the command is established in order to turn it toward manifestation. The reason for their influence over these commands is that Allah the Exalted has deposited [wisdom] within them. And the fifth is the command with which the Spirit was established—for the sake of the manifestation of detail. And the sixth command is that with which intellect was established for the inhabitants of heaven. The seventh is the command with which the celestial images [form of stars] are. And the eighth is the command with which heaven and earth are established. And the ninth command is that with which the divine decrees are established. And the tenth is the command with which heaven and earth are established—after the twelve, and the tenth command is established for subtleties and forms and the resurrection of the existent. The eleventh is the command that intercedes between the people of Paradise and Hell. The twelfth command is for manifestation and returning to its corollaries.

The retreat of this name is extremely great. Streams of wisdom flow from the heart of the reciter, and unveiling is granted. However, its condition is that the heart should be purified from dangers, and one should be occupied in communion at the time of dawn, and after sunset one should be occupied in meditative recitation. Its appointed minister is Sharābīl, who is the antagonistic spirit.
