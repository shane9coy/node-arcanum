Chapter: Virtues of the name "Kabir"

"Kabir" is derived from "Kibriya" (Greatness), and by "Kabir" is meant the perfection of the Essence—just as by "existence" is meant the perfection of the existent. This perfection reverts to His Essence first and originally. Whatever existent has non-existence preceding and following it, and from which it is derived, is imperfect. When a human's age increases, they are also called "kabir" (old), meaning greatness of state, even though human lifespan is limited. Therefore, the eternal everlasting one upon whom non-existence is impossible is the true "Kabir" (Great). Its detail has passed in the discussion of the name "Mutakabbir" (the Self-Grand), and its dhikr (remembrance) is as follows:

In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful. Ṭ O God, You are the Great One, whose Kibriya (Greatness) is sanctified from the common folk and the years, and whose Essence is purified from the annihilation of creatures. You are the Possessor of Kibriya and Perfection. Your most high, purified Essence is transcendent. You are the Great, the Most High, the Generous, the Most Bountiful in generous gifts, the One who dispenses with the very act of asking. I ask You by the perfection of Your Kibriya, and the existence of Your Essence, and the perfection of Your attributes, that You show me the eye that sees through the multitude of human veils, through the observation of the Kibriya of Lordship—so that my heart may increase with the splendor of Your Kibriya, with light, and joy, and awe. O God, clothe me with awe from Your Kibriya that restrains from me the evil of my enemies, and make for me a garment of honor and Your safety, and passage to Your prophets and Your saints. O Kabir, O God.

Whoever recites this dhikr constantly, the Most Generous Lord protects him and elevates his rank.

Chapter: Virtues of the name "Hafiz"

"Hafiz" is that pure Essence which has kept contrary things preserved from one another by His protection—for example, water and fire, because both are contrary. Similarly, moisture and dryness, and heat and cold are opposites of one another. God the Exalted says:
"Indeed, God holds the heavens and the earth, lest they cease." Ṭ
That is, God the Exalted protects the heavens and the earth. And it has been transmitted:
"Indeed, every soul has a protector over it." Ṭ

It is binding upon the spiritual aspirant (salik) to protect the movement and stillness of each of his breaths, and to abandon objection, because when a servant protects his times with contemplation,

the one who recites it remains always in God's preservation and safety:
God the Exalted protects him from the whispers of the outward and the inward. From his solitude, elevation, honor, and the power of preservation are produced. His assigned angel is Gabriel. When the salik performs the litany of this name, the assigned angel descends upon him, and forty rows of assigned angels are under his command. Whoever undertakes [unclear] his service.

If one fears enemies or [unclear], it should be written and kept. If the design of this [name] is inscribed along with the name of the questioner on the last dates of the moon, written on a clay brick and hung, the enemy will flee.
