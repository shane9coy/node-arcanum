Chapter 13

The Letters of Surah al-Fatiha, Their Forms, and Their Accepted Supplications

Know that among these seven letters—fāʾ, thāʾ, dhāl, ẓāʾ, zāʾ, khāʾ, and mīm—some indicate goodness and some indicate evil. Among them is the letter fāʾ, which occurs in the verse "And Allah is Knowing of what you do." One of the names of Allah the Exalted is al-Khabīr (the Knowing). Allah the Exalted said: "And We have certainly beautified the lowest heaven with lamps" and also the verse "Has been made apparent to mankind the love of desires." There is also the letter thāʾ, which indicates honey and reward. Allah the Exalted said: "Allah bears witness that there is no deity except Him" and the witnessed one is Sa'dī, and there is also the verse "And the witnesses, they are alive with their Lord, provided for them." It also indicates drinking, as in the verse "They will drink from cups containing a mixture of camphor." It also indicates healing. The saying of Allah the Exalted is: "And We send down from the Quran that which is a healing and a mercy for the believers." There is also the letter dhāl, which indicates loss and damage for those who take small intellects. One of the names of Allah the Exalted is Ẓāhir (the Manifest). As for the external aspect, it indicates victory, conquest, and manifestation. Allah the Exalted said: "The nature made by Allah upon which He has created mankind" and "The Creator of the heavens and the earth." And there is this verse: "Do you see any cracks?" Allah the Exalted said: "They will enjoy themselves with their spouses." There is also khāʾ and jīm as well, and these are cold letters. Their temperament is that of water and the moon. Jīm is white, long, and has the temperament of preponderance. Khāʾ and shīn are both cold and dry letters. This is the temperament of earth. Qāf and khāʾ have a hot-wet temperament. Fāʾ is hot-dry; its temperament is like fire. It corresponds to hearing, pearls, and the sun. These seven letters are contained in the seven coming names of Allah. They are Jabbār, Jabbār Khābirī's apparent singular, and Shakūr. Some have placed Shahīd instead of Shakūr. Jāʾ has appeared in only Wārith and Bā'is among the names of Allah the Exalted, and even then, it is in the last rank after the realm of negation. It points in Bā'is toward gathering and in Wārith toward self-sufficiency. There is no procession (sulūk) for these two names. Among the letters that form words, there are those letters that have three dots. These are shīn and thāʾ, because shīn encompasses what is external to it, and thāʾ penetrates into what is other than it. The special property of thāʾ belongs only to the realm of lower bodies. It is a dry letter; it is for the earth, as the dead are mountains. Thāʾ is a hot letter that intervenes among the letters of heat. It is in the fifth degree of heat. The form of thāʾ is taken as a model from the letter bāʾ. The table of its number is here.

Whoever wishes to become acquainted with some of the secrets of the letter bāʾ from among the names of Allah should contemplate Fāṭir, Qā'il, Fāliq, Fard, and Fattaḥ. The temperament of the letter shīn is cold, its number is fāʾ, and the secret of thāʾ is the secret of shīn and its confirmation. Among the dotted letters, shīn is the only letter with three marks and three forms. Shīn is the collector of the ranks of pleasure, joy, and delights. The attribute of shīn is in "Allah bears witness," from which three testimonies emanate: one is the testimony of the angels
