[Heading inside a decorative frame]
Explanation of the Beautiful Names
[End of heading]

The name of Allah—glorious and exalted—is the Supreme Name, and it is among the most magnificent dhikrs. Whoever, after a prolonged spiritual discipline (riyāḍah) while fasting, performs two units of optional prayer at midnight, and then recites the dhikr of Allah sixty-six times—a servant called Muwakkal, who is the commander of the servants, shall descend upon him. This servant governs eighty-eight rows of servants, and each row consists of eighty-eight servants under him. When the reciter performs the dhikr of the Name of Allah in seclusion, this servant appears. Before appearing, this servant prostrates before Allah's presence and requests permission in this manner:

Allāhumma innī as'aluka yā Allāhu yā wājidu yā Allāhu yā aḥadu yā Allāhu yā ṣamadu yā Allāhu yā bāqiyu yā Allāhu yā qadīmu yā Allāhu yā qadīru yā Allāhu yā rabbu yā Allāhu yā shakūru yā Allāhu yā ḥayyu yā Allāhu yā qayyūmu as'aluka bi-aḥadiyyatika wa-ṣamadiyyatika wa lāhut rabūbiyyatika anna 'abdan min 'abīdika qad shārakanā fī l-tasbīḥi wa l-taqdīsi wa l-amru amruka fa-in amartanā bi-l-nuzūli ilayhi fa-yā rabbi adhinuka

"O Allah, I ask You—O Allah, O the Finder, O Allah, O the One, O Allah, O the Eternal, O Allah, O the Everlasting, O Allah, O the Ancient, O Allah, O the Powerful, O Allah, O the Lord, O Allah, O the Appreciative, O Allah, O the Living, O Allah, O the Self-Subsisting—I ask You by Your oneness, Your eternity, and the essence of Your lordship, that one of Your servants has included us in glorification and sanctification. The command is Yours; if You command us to descend to him, then with Your will it shall be."

Then Allah—glorious and exalted—speaks to this servant: "Go to My servant and fulfill his need, for he has supplicated with My Supreme Name." Then this servant comes to the reciter along with his companions and says: "O righteous servant, mention Allah." When he performs this dhikr, he sees light emanating from his mouth, and he experiences tranquility and peace. Then that servant says to him: "You have recited the dhikr of the Supreme Name of Allah. We are the servants of this Supreme Name. What do you desire?" The reciter should say: "I desire obedience to Allah and His Names." That servant will tell him: "Keep your body and clothes always pure. Observe fasting three days each week—Thursday, Friday, and Saturday. Break your fast with lawful food. This Name should be recited according to its numerical value. If you do this, we are your brothers." Then that servant will shake hands with you, make a pact, fulfill your need, and then depart.

The Servant of the Name al-Raḥmān and Its Benefits:

Among the great Names of Allah are Allah and al-Raḥmān. Allah—glorious and exalted—has said: "Say, 'Invoke Allah or invoke al-Raḥmān; whichever you invoke, to Him belong the most beautiful names.'" (Qur'an 17:110) However, call upon Allah, or call upon al-Raḥmān—whichever you call upon, all His names are beautiful. The servant of the "Name al-Raḥmān" is Zariyāl, under whom there are four officers, each of whom governs ninety-eight rows, and each row contains ninety-eight servants. They are the servants of mercy. When the reciter performs the dhikr of this Name, its servant—i.e., the mawwal—removes the crown from his head, prostrates before Allah—glorious and exalted—and submits: "O You who knows what is—that a servant has joined us in glorification by Your Name." Then this servant meets the reciter along with his companions, and the gates of mercy and acceptance open upon him. Within this are secrets whose explanation is impossible. His supplication in the morning is this:

Allāhumma innī as'aluka bi-Allāhi yā raḥmānu yā man wasi'at al-mawjūdāti bi-l-ḥayāh al-azaliyyati wa aẓharat asrārahā fī qulūbi ashkhāṣihā bi-l-ataiyā al-sarmadiyyati wa aḥyayta dhawwātahā fī anwārihā bi-l-īrādati l-mutakawwinati li-kay taẓhara bihā sirru l-īrādati wa anta l-raḥmānu bi-tarbīati l-ruḥamā'i wa anta l-qawiyyu li-man fī l-arḍi wa man fī l-samā'i
