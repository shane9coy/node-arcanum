The moon dwells in one of these mansions—that is, from one sunrise to the next sunrise, and at the time of the dawn prayer, it is half in one and half in the other. Similarly, the sun also traverses these mansions in one year.

Know that the Arabs also call the mansions al-anwāʾ, because when one of them sets, another rises with it. Some say that the meaning of anwāʾ is the rising of the mansions and the setting of the stars. Its opposite is that another rises within one hour every night, except for the thirteenth night, and every mansion continues like this for the whole year except for the [one belonging to] July, which has fourteen days. We have not seen the meaning of "nuh" anywhere except as falling in this context. In the pre-Islamic period of Arabia, they attributed rains, winds, cold, and movement to these mansions. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may God be pleased with him, said: "We were rained upon by the rising of such-and-such a star."(1)

Chapter: On the Rulings of the Rising of the Mansions

On the twenty-fifth of Nisan,(2) al-Sharāṭīn rises. The Sun enters Aklīl—that is, it rises on the last night of Nisan. Thurayyā, when it is in Hūt, sets at the time of Āyār in the west and then remains hidden for fifty nights. Then it rises in the east at the time of morning. When it reaches the middle of the sky with the setting of the Sun, severe cold occurs, and blight departs from the fruits. The noble Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "When the star rises, blight is removed from the fruits." Debarān rises on the eighth of Ḥazīrān. Haqiʿah on the twenty-first. Al-Dhirāʿ on the fourth of Tammūz. And Nathrah on the seventeenth. At the same time, a star called Shuʿara al-ʿAbūr rises. On the first of August, Jabhah on the fourteenth. Zabraʾ on the twenty-seventh. Sarat on the eighth of Elūl. ʿAwāʾ on the nineteenth. And Sumāk, while two nights of Elūl remain, rises. Ghafar on the eleventh of Tishrīn al-Awwal. Zubanāʾ on the twenty-sixth. Aklīl on the seventeenth. Qalb on the nineteenth. And Shawlah on the first of Kānūn al-Awwal. Naʿāʾim on the fourteenth. Baladah on the twenty-seventh. Dhābiḥ on the ninth of Kānūn al-Thānī. Saʿd Bulaʿ on the twenty-second. Saʿd al-Suʿūd on the fourth of Shubāṭ. Saʿd al-Akhbiyah on the seventeenth. Farrʿ Muqaddam after two nights have passed. Farrʿ Mu'akhkhar on the twenty-fourth. And Rishāʾ rises on the fourth of Nisan.

[Title within a decorative rectangular frame:]

Chapter: The Distribution of the Mansions over the Four Seasons

The mansions of the spring season are: al-Sharāṭīn, al-Bitayn, Thurayyā, Debarān, Haqiʿah, Hawniʿah, and al-Dhirāʿ. The mansions of the summer season are: Nathrah, Ṭarf, Jabhah, Zabraʾ, Sarat, Sumāk, and ʿAwāʾ. The mansions of the autumn season are: Ghafar, Zubanāʾ, Aklīl, Qalb, Shawlah, Naʿāʾim, and Baladah. The mansions of the winter season are: Saʿd al-Dhābiḥ, Saʿd Bulaʿ, Saʿd al-Suʿūd, Saʿd al-Akhbiyah, Farrʿ Muqaddam, Farrʿ Mu'akhkhar, and Rishāʾ. Each season has seven mansions.

[Margin separated by a horizontal line:]

(1) Although this is prohibited according to the Sacred Law, and attributing such a statement to ʿUmar is incompatible with his dignity.

(2) Nisan, Elūl, etc., are months of the ancient Roman calendar.
