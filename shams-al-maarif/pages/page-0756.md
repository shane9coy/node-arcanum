Chapter: The names of awe and majesty—the unjust are dispersed or destroyed through their mention:

These are a few names of awe and majesty. Through them the courage and the gatherings of the unjust are dispersed. Every sincere one who makes supplication with them, God Most High averts from him every cruelty, and whoever among the unjust hears the mention of these names is destroyed. Whoever recites them before a tyrant and oppressor, that oppressor becomes his submissive servant. These names are:

Al-ʿAzīz, Al-Qahhār, Al-Muqaddir, Al-Qawī, Al-Qāʾim, Dhū Al-Quwwah, Al-Matīn, Al-Naṣīr, Al-Jabbār, Al-Mutakabbir, Al-Shadīd, Al-Qāhir, Al-Qahhār, Al-Qāʾim.

There are two possibilities regarding the names Qāʾim and Qayyūm: either they are action names (asmāʾ al-fiʿl) or they are essence names (asmāʾ al-dhāt). When they are action names, their meanings pertain to administration (tadbīr)—that is, when a person administers something, the Arabs say that this person is qāʾim and qayyūm over that thing. When their meanings pertain to the essence (dhāt), then they denote the self-subsisting, independent of others. In any case, both are among the attributes of the essence name. The difference between qāʾim and qayyūm is that qāʾim is one who stands for the protection or care of others, as is understood from this verse:

"Is he who is qāʾim over every soul for what it has earned?"

What is the difference between qāʾim and qayyūm:

And in this verse it states: "qāʾiman bil-qist"—that is, He stands with justice over His creation. The meaning of qayyūm is that He stands by His own essence, and all things are in need of Him, just as creation is in need of the Creator. This is the difference between qāʾim and qayyūm. And [its weight] is its object (mafʿūl), and from it is derived. The weight of qāʾim is fāʿil, and the name is qayyūm because the Lord Most High alone is qāʾim bi-nafsihi in existence; there is no other [qāʾim] besides Him. And it is also necessary that He stands by His own power, and all things are in need of Him for their existence. [The subjugation] before Him—[the key]—that is, before His knowledge and will and power and hearing and sight. Thus it is known that He alone is free from every attribute and is [the one who gathers], the Knowing and the Wise.

"My Lord, deliver me from the sea of Your awe, until I emerge [firmly established], outwardly and inwardly, [against everyone who intends evil for me]."
