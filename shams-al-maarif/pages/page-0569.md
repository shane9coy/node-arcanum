Then preserve this distillate, and take one hundred and one uqiya, and pour half an uqiya of the aforementioned distillate into it, and place it in a bottle whose width and length is 13 fingers less than one span, and whose height is one span, and the lid should also be of the same glass. Then seal the lid of this bottle with sealing clay and place this bottle in a cauldron that can be placed on a stove, and fill it with water such that the entire bottle is submerged—only the sealing clay part remains above. Then light a fire under this cauldron and watch carefully that the sealing clay does not break anywhere allowing it to escape; all the calx is waiting for this, and also watch the fire so that all the water in the cauldron dries up and the bottle breaks. Then when you see that the thing inside the bottle is completely dry, at that time extinguish the fire, and after the water cools, open the bottle and then pour one tullāq of the aforementioned distillate into it, and repeat this process until rainbow colors appear in it, and each time add distillate equal to one tullāq. Then when it becomes prepared with this quality, place one dirham upon ten of silver or gold as you wish, and make a kuhl from it.

The gold-making or silver-making elixir: But regarding this kuhl upon which copper is placed, or if the kuhl is for gold, then gold will be produced, and if for silver, then silver will be produced—and for the purpose of placing [on metals], all these qualities are equal. Silver is "white"; copper is "red"; tin is "rank"—whichever serves the purpose of placing upon it.

The second method for making elixir: Another operation is described, which is my separate method, and they call it "the parrot." The philosophers-kings used to perform this operation, and due to its ease, it is suitable only for them. Its composition is as follows: Mars, purified from Mars, free from defect—grind both of these. Moon, Libra—grind both; Sun—grind and mix the first ground [materials] together so that all become one body. After that, from the second table, grind Moon—2, Sun—1, and Venus—3 and Mars—4, place all of the first [set] upon it, combine all the small particles into one mass, and then grind both collections and make them one. Then dissolve them with quicksilver (qitrūn), grind finely to equal weight, then add from it this time, then apply it—it will become correct. The balance is as follows:

The method for using iron: The method of using Mars is as follows: mix one jūtīyā of iron filings, equal weight, with purified quicksilver, and add one dirham of verdigris and lemon distillate, and dissolve well. When it becomes correct, add half a jūtīyā of sal ammoniac and two-thirds of saffron, then dissolve. Bāz-jāz, sal ammoniac, and natrun should be sprinkled from above, then it will be ready for the gāū operation.

Spirit, meaning glass—this is a purification rule: heat it in fire until red, then quench it in strong vinegar; it will become mixed.

The composition of tin is as follows: take equal weight of inland salt, dissolve it in egg white, place it in an earthenware vessel over fire, and when it melts, remove it and make it into pills, then repeat the operation until it becomes free from impurity.
